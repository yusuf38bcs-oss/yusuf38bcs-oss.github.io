<p class="lbfl-academic-lead">Use this guide with any LBFL lesson, assessment, practical, or reflection. The goal is not to memorize another framework. It is to move through one repeatable learning cycle until you can explain the biology, use it, inspect your reasoning, and correct what is still weak.</p>

<div class="lbfl-academic-callout" role="note">
  <p><strong>This guide owns the learning method, not the subject content.</strong> Course pages still own sequence, lessons still own topic-specific objectives and explanations, MCQ/CQ surfaces own full assessment, practical pages own procedures and observations, and reflective pages keep their existing educational boundaries.</p>
</div>

<h2>The six-stage LBFL learning cycle</h2>

<ol class="lbfl-academic-stepper" data-learning-cycle="lbfl-v1">
  <li data-learning-stage="Understand">
    <h3>Understand</h3>
    <p>Build an accurate model of the concept, mechanism, structure-function relationship, or evidence before trying to memorize details.</p>
    <p><strong>Check:</strong> What is happening, why does it happen, and which evidence or relationship makes the explanation biologically valid?</p>
  </li>
  <li data-learning-stage="Retrieve">
    <h3>Retrieve</h3>
    <p>Close the source briefly and recall, predict, label, calculate, or choose an answer from memory. Retrieval exposes what is available without rereading.</p>
    <p><strong>Check:</strong> What can you produce before looking back?</p>
  </li>
  <li data-learning-stage="Explain">
    <h3>Explain</h3>
    <p>State the mechanism or relationship in your own words. Use correct biological terms, but do not hide weak understanding behind copied wording.</p>
    <p><strong>Check:</strong> Can you connect cause, structure, process, and outcome clearly?</p>
  </li>
  <li data-learning-stage="Apply">
    <h3>Apply</h3>
    <p>Transfer the concept to a diagram, stimulus, specimen, practical observation, data set, unfamiliar example, or real-life biological situation.</p>
    <p><strong>Check:</strong> Can the same idea solve a new problem rather than only reproduce the original example?</p>
  </li>
  <li data-learning-stage="Reflect">
    <h3>Reflect</h3>
    <p>Inspect uncertainty, misconceptions, evidence quality, and the learning strategy you used. Reflection describes what happened; it does not assign a fixed ability or identity.</p>
    <p><strong>Check:</strong> Where did the reasoning become uncertain, incomplete, or overconfident?</p>
  </li>
  <li data-learning-stage="Repair">
    <h3>Repair</h3>
    <p>Return to the source concept, rebuild the mistaken step, then reattempt retrieval or application. A corrected answer matters most when the corrected reasoning can be used again.</p>
    <p><strong>Check:</strong> What exactly changed in your explanation after feedback?</p>
  </li>
</ol>

<h2>Two supporting loops already used in LBFL</h2>

<div class="lbfl-academic-comparison">
  <section class="lbfl-academic-card">
    <h3>Synaptic Bridge</h3>
    <p><strong>Observe → Question → Connect → Explain</strong></p>
    <p>This critical-thinking loop helps you build and communicate an evidence-based explanation. It sits mainly inside <strong>Understand</strong> and <strong>Explain</strong>.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lbfl-academic-card">
    <h3>Assessment repair loop</h3>
    <p><strong>Attempt → Feedback → Repair → Reattempt</strong></p>
    <p>This feedback loop makes retrieval useful by sending a wrong or weak answer back to its source concept before another attempt.</p>
  </section>
</div>

<h2>Use the cycle without adding more boilerplate</h2>

<div class="lbfl-academic-flow" aria-label="Learning guide use across an LBFL study session">
  <div><strong>Before the lesson</strong><br>Read the topic-specific objective and identify what you need to understand.</div>
  <div><strong>During the lesson</strong><br>Retrieve, explain, and apply the concept through the task the lesson provides.</div>
  <div><strong>After feedback</strong><br>Reflect on the error, return to the source, repair the model, and reattempt.</div>
</div>

<div class="lbfl-academic-evidence">
  <h2>What remains on individual pages</h2>
  <p>Topic-specific objectives, outcomes, scientific explanations, figures, concise retrieval prompts, practical observations, assessment feedback, references, and reflection prompts remain with the page role that owns them. Later convergence work may replace repeated method explanations with a short link to this guide, but it should not delete useful topic-specific teaching structure.</p>
</div>
