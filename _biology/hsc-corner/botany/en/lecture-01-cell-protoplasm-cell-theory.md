  <h1>Cell, Protoplasm and Cell Theory</h1>
  <p class="lead">This English mirror explains the cell as the basic unit of life, protoplasm as living substance, cytoplasm, nucleus, unicellular and multicellular organisms, and the major statements of cell theory.</p>

<section class="lbfl-card summary"><h2>Lesson Summary</h2><p>A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Protoplasm is the living substance of the cell. At HSC level, protoplasm is usually explained as the living part that includes cytoplasm and nucleus. Cell theory explains that living cellular organisms are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of life, and new cells arise from pre-existing cells.</p></section>
<section class="lbfl-card boundary"><h2>Educational Boundary</h2><p>This lesson is prepared for academic biology learning and HSC Biology preparation. The language is simplified, but the biological meaning is kept accurate. Students should still follow their textbook, teacher and institutional guideline for syllabus-specific wording.</p></section>

<section class="lbfl-card"><h2>Main Lesson Body</h2><p>Cells are the basic structural and functional units of life. Every cellular organism is made of one or more cells. A unicellular organism performs all life activities within one cell, while a multicellular organism depends on many specialized cells working together.</p><p>The living substance inside the cell is called protoplasm. In HSC Biology, protoplasm is usually explained as the living part of the cell that includes cytoplasm and nucleus. Cytoplasm is the region between the plasma membrane and the nucleus. The nucleus stores genetic information and helps control cell activities.</p><p>Cell theory explains the cellular foundation of life. Its major statements are: all living cellular organisms are made of one or more cells; the cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life; and new cells arise from pre-existing cells.</p></section>

<section><h2>Glossary</h2><div class="grid"><div class="term"><strong>Cell</strong><span>The basic structural and functional unit of life.</span></div><div class="term"><strong>Protoplasm</strong><span>The living substance of the cell. At HSC level: protoplasm = cytoplasm + nucleus.</span></div><div class="term"><strong>Cytoplasm</strong><span>The region outside the nucleus and inside the plasma membrane.</span></div><div class="term"><strong>Cell Theory</strong><span>A foundational biological theory explaining the cellular basis of life.</span></div><div class="term"><strong>Cytology</strong><span>The branch of biology that studies cells.</span></div><div class="term"><strong>Unicellular Organism</strong><span>An organism made of one cell.</span></div><div class="term"><strong>Multicellular Organism</strong><span>An organism made of many cells.</span></div></div></section>

<section class="lbfl-card objectives"><h2>Learning Objectives</h2><ul class="lbfl-list"><li>Define cell in simple biological language.</li><li>Explain why the cell is called the structural and functional unit of life.</li><li>Distinguish between protoplasm and cytoplasm.</li><li>State the major points of cell theory.</li><li>Differentiate between unicellular and multicellular organisms.</li></ul></section>
<section class="lbfl-card outcomes"><h2>Learning Outcomes</h2><ul class="lbfl-list"><li>Students can explain cell as the basic unit of life.</li><li>Students can explain protoplasm = cytoplasm + nucleus.</li><li>Students can state the three major statements of cell theory.</li><li>Students can explain how Amoeba performs life activities as a unicellular organism.</li></ul></section>
<section class="lbfl-dlc dot"><h2>DOT — Concept Clarity</h2><p>A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. “Structural unit” means living bodies are built from cells. “Functional unit” means basic life activities occur through cells.</p><p>Protoplasm is the living substance of the cell. At HSC level, it is commonly explained as cytoplasm plus nucleus.</p><p><strong>Core formula:</strong> Protoplasm = Cytoplasm + Nucleus</p><p>The major statements of cell theory are: all cellular organisms are made of one or more cells; the cell is the basic unit of life; new cells arise from pre-existing cells.</p></section>
<section class="lbfl-dlc line"><h2>LINE — Application and Connection</h2><p>Cell theory connects nearly every later topic in biology. Tissues are groups of similar cells; organs are organized groups of tissues; genetics studies hereditary information stored in cells; and cell division explains how new cells arise.</p><p>The idea of protoplasm prevents students from thinking of the cell as an empty box. A cell contains living material, chemical reactions, genetic information, organelles, membranes and energy systems.</p></section>
<section class="lbfl-dlc circle"><h2>CIRCLE — Life Connection</h2><p>The cell teaches that large living systems are built from small organized units. Learning also begins with small clear concepts. One clear concept becomes a DOT; connected concepts become a LINE; repeated understanding becomes a CIRCLE.</p></section>
<section class="lbfl-thinking"><h2>Deep Thinking Box</h2><p>Do not ask only: What is a cell?</p><p><strong>Ask also:</strong> Why can one cell be alive?</p><p>A cell is alive because it has organization, boundary, chemical activity, genetic information and the ability to respond.</p></section>

<section><h2>Common Mistakes</h2><div class="mistake"><b>Mistake:</b> Cells are found only in animals. <strong>Correction:</strong> Cells are found in plants, animals, fungi, protists and bacteria.</div><div class="mistake"><b>Mistake:</b> Protoplasm means only cytoplasm. <strong>Correction:</strong> At HSC level, protoplasm usually includes cytoplasm and nucleus.</div><div class="mistake"><b>Mistake:</b> All organisms are multicellular. <strong>Correction:</strong> Many organisms, such as Amoeba, are unicellular.</div><div class="mistake"><b>Mistake:</b> Cell theory says cells are created suddenly. <strong>Correction:</strong> New cells arise from pre-existing cells.</div></section>

<section id="cell-protoplasm-cell-theory-cq-practice"><h2>Cell, Protoplasm and Cell Theory CQ Practice</h2><article class="lbfl-cq-card"><h3>CQ Stimulus</h3><p>A student observed Amoeba under a microscope. The teacher explained that Amoeba is made of only one cell, yet it can move, take food, respire, excrete and reproduce. Later, the same student observed plant tissue and found many cells arranged together.</p><details open><summary>Questions</summary><p><strong>a.</strong> What is a cell?</p><p><strong>b.</strong> Why is protoplasm called the living substance of the cell?</p><p><strong>c.</strong> Explain how Amoeba performs life activities as a unicellular organism.</p><p><strong>d.</strong> Analyze why cell theory is important for understanding multicellular organisms.</p></details><details><summary>Model Answer</summary><p><strong>a.</strong> A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.</p><p><strong>b.</strong> Protoplasm is called the living substance because it contains active living material needed for cellular activities. At HSC level, it includes cytoplasm and nucleus.</p><p><strong>c.</strong> Amoeba is unicellular. Its single cell performs movement, nutrition, respiration, excretion and reproduction. The cytoplasm supports internal reactions, the plasma membrane controls exchange, and the nucleus controls activities.</p><p><strong>d.</strong> Cell theory shows that multicellular organisms are built from cells. Specialized cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs form organ systems. Thus the foundation of a large body is cellular organization.</p></details></article></section>
