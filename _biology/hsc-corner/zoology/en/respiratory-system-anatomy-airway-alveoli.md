# Human Respiratory System Part 1: Airway, Lungs and Alveoli

This English mirror explains the first part of the Human Respiratory System series. The focus is the pathway of air from the outside environment to the alveoli and the structural logic that makes gas exchange possible.

## Air Route Master Diagram

```text
External air
  ↓
Anterior nostril
  ↓
Vestibule
  ↓
Nasal cavity
  ↓
Posterior nasal aperture / Choana
  ↓
Nasopharynx → Oropharynx
  ↓
Larynx
  ↓
Trachea
  ↓
Right and left primary bronchus
  ↓
Secondary bronchus → Segmental bronchus
  ↓
Bronchiole → Terminal bronchiole → Respiratory bronchiole
  ↓
Alveolar duct → Alveolar sac → Alveoli
  ↓
Gas exchange: oxygen enters blood, carbon dioxide enters alveoli
```

## Core Ideas

The respiratory tract does not simply carry air. It filters, warms, moistens, conducts, protects, and finally brings air to a thin gas-exchange surface. The nose and nasal cavity condition air. The larynx protects the airway and supports voice. The trachea and bronchi keep the route open. The bronchioles regulate airflow. The alveoli provide the large, thin, moist surface needed for diffusion.

## Learning Outcomes

After this lesson, learners should be able to draw the air route, distinguish upper and lower respiratory tract, explain the function of tracheal cartilage, describe the bronchial tree, and justify why the alveolus is the functional unit of the lung.

## Synaptic Bridge

The respiratory pathway is a model of efficient design: each station prepares the next step. In learning, the same principle applies. Strong foundations make advanced mechanisms easier to understand.
