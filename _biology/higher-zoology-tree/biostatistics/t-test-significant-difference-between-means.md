## Concept Overview

A **t-test** compares one or more means while accounting for uncertainty in an estimated standard deviation. The correct version depends on the study design.

| Design | Suitable procedure |
|---|---|
| one sample vs reference mean | one-sample t-test |
| two independent groups | Welch or pooled two-sample t-test |
| matched/before-after observations | paired t-test |

For two independent groups, **Welch's t-test** is a robust default when equal variances are not established.

## Assumptions

The observations should be generated by a design that supports independence within the relevant sampling units. For small samples, strong skewness or extreme outliers can undermine the usual t-model. A paired design must be analyzed as paired data.

## Welch Two-Sample Formula

```text
t = (X̄₁ − X̄₂) / √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂)
```

The degrees of freedom are estimated with the Welch–Satterthwaite equation rather than simply using n₁+n₂−2.

## Worked Example

| Group | n | Mean yield | SD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Variety A | 10 | 42 kg | 4 kg |
| Variety B | 10 | 37 kg | 5 kg |

```text
Difference = 42 − 37 = 5 kg

SE = √(4²/10 + 5²/10)
   = √4.1
   ≈ 2.025

t = 5 / 2.025
  ≈ 2.47
```

Using Welch's degrees-of-freedom approximation:

```text
df ≈ 16.99
```

For a two-sided test this t statistic corresponds to a p-value of roughly 0.024. Under the model, the data therefore provide evidence against equal population means at α = 0.05.

The estimated mean difference is **5 kg**. Statistical evidence should still be interpreted alongside a confidence interval, study design, sample size and biological importance.

## Pooled t-test

A pooled two-sample t-test uses a common variance estimate and is justified only when the equal-variance model is scientifically and statistically defensible. It is not interchangeable with Welch's method without stating the assumption.

## Statistical vs Biological Importance

A small effect may be statistically detectable with enough information, while a biologically important effect may remain imprecisely estimated in a small study. Report the effect estimate and uncertainty rather than only a significance label.

## Common Errors

- analyzing paired observations as independent;
- silently switching between Welch and pooled formulas;
- saying a non-significant result proves “no effect”;
- treating p < 0.05 as evidence of causation;
- omitting effect size and uncertainty.
