# Genetics Lecture 02: Genetic Terminology

## Concept Overview

Genetics শেখার সবচেয়ে বড় বাধা হলো terminology confusion. Gene, allele, locus, genotype, phenotype, homozygous, heterozygous, dominant, recessive—এই শব্দগুলো পরিষ্কার না হলে Mendelian ratio, Punnett square, test cross, pedigree or gene interaction বোঝা কঠিন হয়ে যায়।

এই lecture হলো Genetics vocabulary map. এখানে প্রতিটি term শুধু সংজ্ঞা হিসেবে নয়, inheritance reasoning-এর tool হিসেবে শেখানো হয়েছে।

## Why This Matters

Genetics problems সাধারণত শব্দ দিয়ে শুরু হয়, ratio দিয়ে শেষ হয়। যদি learner বুঝতে না পারে কোন parent homozygous, কোন trait recessive, কোন genotype heterozygous, কোন phenotype visible, তাহলে calculation ঠিক হলেও biological interpretation ভুল হতে পারে। তাই terminology হলো Genetics problem-solving-এর grammar.

## Core Vocabulary Map

<div class="lbfl-info-grid">
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Gene</h3><p>DNA-এর functional unit that influences a trait.</p><p><strong>Use:</strong> explains inherited biological information.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Allele</h3><p>একই gene-এর alternative form.</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> T and t for plant height.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Locus</h3><p>Chromosome-এ gene-এর নির্দিষ্ট অবস্থান।</p><p><strong>Use:</strong> tells where a gene is located.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Character</h3><p>Broad heritable feature.</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> seed shape, plant height.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Trait</h3><p>Character-এর specific form.</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> round seed, dwarf plant.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Factor</h3><p>Mendel's historical term for hereditary unit; now understood as gene/allelic factor.</p></section>
</div>

## Genotype and Phenotype

<div class="lbfl-info-grid lbfl-info-grid--compact">
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Genotype</h3><p>Allele combination or genetic constitution.</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> TT, Tt, tt.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Phenotype</h3><p>Observable or measurable expression.</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> tall or dwarf.</p></section>
</div>

```text
Genotype + Environment + Developmental context
  ↓
Phenotype
```

## Homozygous and Heterozygous

| Term | Meaning | Example | Learning point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homozygous dominant | two dominant alleles | TT | true-breeding dominant possible |
| Heterozygous | two different alleles | Tt | dominant phenotype may appear |
| Homozygous recessive | two recessive alleles | tt | recessive phenotype appears |

## Dominant and Recessive

<div class="lbfl-info-grid lbfl-info-grid--compact">
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Dominant allele</h3><p>Heterozygous condition-এ phenotype প্রকাশ করতে পারে।</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> T in Tt gives tall phenotype.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Recessive allele</h3><p>Dominant allele উপস্থিত থাকলে masked থাকে; homozygous condition-এ প্রকাশ পায়।</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> tt gives dwarf phenotype.</p></section>
</div>

Important: Recessive allele does not disappear. It can be hidden in heterozygous condition and reappear in later generations.

## Pure Line and Hybrid

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Pure line | genetically stable true-breeding line | TT tall line or tt dwarf line |
| Hybrid | offspring from genetically different parents | Tt from TT × tt |
| Monohybrid | hybrid for one character | Tt for plant height |
| Dihybrid | hybrid for two characters | RrYy for seed shape and colour |

## Parental, F1 and F2 Generations

```text
P generation = parental generation
  ↓ cross
F1 generation = first filial generation
  ↓ selfing / crossing
F2 generation = second filial generation
```

These terms describe generation sequence in genetic crosses.

## Test Cross and Back Cross

<div class="lbfl-info-grid lbfl-info-grid--compact">
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Test cross</h3><p>Unknown dominant phenotype individual is crossed with homozygous recessive individual to identify genotype.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Back cross</h3><p>F1 individual is crossed with one of its parents or parent-like genotype.</p></section>
</div>

Test cross logic:

```text
Unknown tall plant = TT or Tt?
  ↓ cross with tt
All tall offspring → likely TT
Tall + dwarf offspring → likely Tt
```

## Term Relationship Flow

```text
Gene located at locus
  ↓
Gene has alleles
  ↓
Allele pair forms genotype
  ↓
Genotype contributes to phenotype
  ↓
Phenotype appears as trait
  ↓
Traits are followed through generations
```

## Common Confusions

<div class="lbfl-info-grid">
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Gene vs Allele</h3><p>Gene is the unit; allele is a version of that gene.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Character vs Trait</h3><p>Character is the category; trait is the specific form.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Genotype vs Phenotype</h3><p>Genotype is genetic constitution; phenotype is expressed result.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Dominant vs Common</h3><p>Dominant does not always mean more common in population.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Recessive vs Weak</h3><p>Recessive does not mean biologically weak; it means masked in heterozygous state.</p></section>
  <section class="lbfl-info-card"><h3>Test cross vs Selfing</h3><p>Test cross identifies genotype; selfing follows inheritance pattern within same line.</p></section>
</div>

## Worked Mini Example

A pea plant is tall. Tallness is dominant over dwarfness. The plant may be **TT** or **Tt**. To identify its genotype, cross it with a dwarf plant **tt**.

```text
If TT × tt → all Tt → all tall
If Tt × tt → Tt and tt → tall and dwarf
```

So offspring pattern helps reveal the hidden genotype.

## Synaptic Bridge

Genetic terminology teaches precision. One word can change the whole meaning of a problem. In life and leadership too, vague language creates wrong decisions; precise language builds clear thinking.

## Critical Thinking Questions

1. Why is allele not exactly the same as gene?
2. How can two plants have the same phenotype but different genotype?
3. Why does recessive allele reappear in later generations?
4. How does test cross reveal hidden genotype?
5. Why is dominant not always equal to common?
