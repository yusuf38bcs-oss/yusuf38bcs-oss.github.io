# Bufo

### The Puzzle

A toad is neither an “unfinished fish” nor an “incomplete reptile.” It combines a terrestrial adult lifestyle with water-dependent reproductive and physiological features.

---

## 1. Systematic position

Traditional NU teaching uses **Bufo** for true toads. Many South Asian species historically placed in *Bufo* are now classified differently.

Chordata → Vertebrata → Amphibia → Anura → Bufonidae → traditional *Bufo*.

---

## 2. Amphibian diagnostic features

- ectothermic tetrapods;
- moist glandular skin;
- lungs plus important cutaneous respiration;
- three-chambered heart;
- anamniotic eggs;
- water/moisture-linked reproduction;
- aquatic larva common;
- metamorphosis common.

---

## 3. External morphology

Adult toads have a head and trunk, no adult tail, strong hindlimbs, eyelids, tympanum, nostrils, a broad mouth, rough glandular skin and often prominent parotoid glands.

![Bufo body plan](/assets/biology/animal-diversity/lecture-07/lecture-07-bufo-body-plan.svg)

---

## 4. Skin as a physiological organ

The moist vascular skin contributes to gas exchange and water balance. Mucus glands help maintain the wet diffusion surface; granular glands provide chemical defense.

---

## 5. Locomotion and skeleton

Limbs, strong girdles and a reinforced axial skeleton support the body on land. Hindlimbs generate hopping forces.

---

## 6. Feeding and digestion

Adults are largely insectivorous/carnivorous and use a rapidly protrusible sticky tongue.

```text
Mouth → esophagus → stomach → intestine → large intestine → cloaca
```

---

## 7. Respiration

Adults combine:

- **cutaneous respiration** through moist skin;
- **buccopharyngeal respiration**;
- **pulmonary respiration** using positive-pressure buccal pumping.

There is no mammalian diaphragm-driven negative-pressure mechanism.

---

## 8. Heart and double circulation

The heart has two atria and one ventricle plus sinus venosus/outflow regions.

Blood moves through systemic and pulmocutaneous circuits. Separation is incomplete but functionally organized.

---

## 9. Portal circulation

A portal system carries blood from one capillary network through a vein to a second capillary network.

![Bufo portal systems](/assets/biology/animal-diversity/lecture-07/lecture-07-bufo-portal-system.svg)

#### Hepatic portal
gut/spleen → hepatic portal vein → liver sinusoids → hepatic veins → systemic venous return.

#### Renal portal
hindlimb/posterior body → renal portal veins → kidney capillaries → renal veins/systemic return.

Portal systems enable organ-specific processing before blood returns to the heart.

---

## 10. Excretion

Adult amphibians possess functional kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder and cloaca. Adults are mainly ureotelic, whereas aquatic larvae may excrete more ammonia.

---

## 11. Nervous and sensory systems

Important terrestrial adaptations include eyelids, tympanum/middle ear and well-developed olfaction and balance systems.

---

## 12. Reproduction

In typical anurans:

- sexes are separate;
- amplexus occurs;
- eggs are released into water;
- fertilization is usually external;
- jelly-coated anamniotic eggs develop into tadpoles.

The absence of an amniotic shell is a central reason reproduction remains water-dependent.

---

## 13. Tadpole metamorphosis

Tadpoles are aquatic, tailed and gill-breathing. During metamorphosis:

- limbs develop;
- tail is resorbed;
- gills disappear;
- lungs become dominant;
- the gut remodels;
- sensory and cranial systems reorganize.

Thyroid hormones regulate the metamorphic program.

---

## 14. Living Amphibia

![Living Amphibia classification](/assets/biology/animal-diversity/lecture-07/lecture-07-amphibia-classification.svg)

- **Anura:** frogs and toads;
- **Caudata/Urodela:** salamanders and newts;
- **Gymnophiona/Apoda:** caecilians.

---

## 15. Adaptive radiation and neoteny

Amphibians occupy aquatic, terrestrial, arboreal and burrowing niches.

**Neoteny** is the retention of juvenile/larval characters after reproductive maturity, as seen in famous salamander examples.

---

## 16. Section A — one-mark capsule

**Portal system:** venous pathway connecting one capillary bed to a second capillary bed.

**Aestivation:** reduced activity during hot/dry unfavorable conditions.

**Neoteny:** retention of larval traits in a sexually mature animal.

**Tympanum:** membrane transmitting airborne vibration to the middle ear.

**Amplexus:** anuran breeding embrace.

## 19. Common mistakes

- describing mammalian alveolar lungs;
- calling the renal portal vein an artery;
- calling hepatic portal blood oxygenated arterial blood;
- giving tadpoles only lung respiration;
- giving amphibian eggs an amniotic shell;
- classifying caecilians as reptiles.

---

## 20. Quick revision

| Feature | Bufo |
|---|---|
| class | Amphibia |
| order | Anura |
| skin | moist/glandular |
| respiration | skin + buccopharynx + lungs |
| heart | 2 atria + 1 ventricle |
| portals | hepatic + renal |
| adult waste | mainly urea |
| fertilization | usually external |
| larva | tadpole |

---

## 21. Research-backed update notes

The three living amphibian orders are Anura, Caudata and Gymnophiona. Frog renal portal supply is anatomically documented. Modern taxonomy has changed the names of several species historically taught under *Bufo*.
