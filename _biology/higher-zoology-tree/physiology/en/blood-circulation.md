# Blood Circulation Master Series: Blood, Heart, Circulation, Disease and Heart Health

## Concept Overview

Blood circulation is the transport network of life. Blood carries oxygen, carbon dioxide, nutrients, hormones, enzymes, wastes, heat, immune cells and clotting factors. The heart works as the pump, arteries and veins act as the main transport routes, capillaries serve as exchange points, and lymph returns excess tissue fluid while supporting immunity.

This English version mirrors the Bangla Blood Circulation series in a learner-friendly sequence: **blood composition → corpuscle function → heart structure → cardiac cycle → systemic and pulmonary circulation → disease logic → treatment awareness → healthy living**.

> **Learning boundary:** This series is for education only. It is not medical diagnosis, prescription, emergency instruction, surgery advice or patient-specific treatment guidance. For real symptoms, consult a qualified physician or cardiologist.

## Why This Matters

Blood circulation connects almost every physiological system. The respiratory system loads oxygen into blood. The digestive system supplies nutrients to blood. The kidney filters blood. Endocrine glands release hormones into blood. Immune cells travel through blood and lymph. The heart keeps this transport moving. Therefore, circulation is the central logistics system of human physiology.

## Master Flowchart

```text
Blood = Plasma + Blood Corpuscles
  ↓
Plasma transports water, proteins, nutrients, hormones, wastes and CO₂
  ↓
RBC transports oxygen mainly through hemoglobin
  ↓
WBC supports defence and immunity
  ↓
Platelets support hemostasis and clot formation
  ↓
Heart pumps blood through vessels
  ↓
Pulmonary circulation oxygenates blood in lungs
  ↓
Systemic circulation delivers blood to tissues
  ↓
Capillaries exchange materials with tissue fluid
  ↓
Lymph returns excess tissue fluid and supports immune surveillance
```

## LOLO: Learning Objectives and Learning Outcomes

### Learning Objectives

After completing this series, learners will study how to:

1. Define blood as a fluid connective tissue.
2. Explain plasma and formed elements.
3. Describe the roles of RBC, WBC, platelets and lymph.
4. Label heart chambers, valves and major vessels.
5. Explain systole, diastole and the cardiac cycle.
6. Trace the myogenic impulse pathway: SAN → AVN → Bundle of His → Purkinje fibres.
7. Compare systemic and pulmonary circulation.
8. Analyse angina and heart attack as failures of coronary blood flow.
9. Understand the educational logic of CABG, angioplasty, stents, open-heart surgery and rehabilitation.
10. Apply cardiovascular biology to healthy living.

### Learning Outcomes

By the end of this series, learners will be able to:

- Draw a blood composition tree.
- Prepare a cell-function table for RBC, WBC, platelets and plasma.
- Label RA, RV, LA, LV, valves, aorta, vena cava, pulmonary artery and pulmonary vein.
- Explain the cardiac impulse pathway.
- Trace systemic and pulmonary circulation.
- Distinguish angina from myocardial infarction.
- Compare CABG, PCI/angioplasty, stent placement and open-heart surgery.
- Convert cardiovascular physiology into daily health discipline.

## LALA: Learning Activities and Learning Applications

### Learning Activities

1. Draw a blood composition tree.
2. Draw the heart-flow route using two colours: deoxygenated and oxygenated blood.
3. Build a cardiac impulse pathway map.
4. Compare angina and heart attack in a two-column table.
5. Create a procedure comparison chart for medicine, angioplasty, stent, CABG and open-heart surgery.

### Learning Applications

- Understand why anaemia causes fatigue.
- Explain why coronary artery blockage causes chest pain or heart attack.
- Recognise that treatment restores flow but prevention protects the vessel wall.
- Build a personal heart-health checklist based on food, movement, sleep, tobacco avoidance, blood pressure, glucose and cholesterol monitoring.

## Synaptic Bridge

Blood circulation teaches a life principle: survival depends on open pathways, disciplined rhythm, reliable transport and timely correction. In the body, blocked vessels threaten tissue survival. In life, blocked habits and delayed correction also create damage. Biology therefore becomes a practical guide for responsibility.
