# Blood Circulation Part 1: Blood, Plasma, Blood Corpuscles and Immunity

## Concept Overview

Blood is a fluid connective tissue. It is slightly alkaline, red in colour and continuously flows through the heart, arteries, veins and capillaries. Blood is not only a red transport fluid; it is a coordinated system for transport, defence, repair and regulation.

Blood has two major parts: **plasma** and **formed elements**. Plasma is the liquid matrix. Formed elements include red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets. These components work together to transport oxygen, carbon dioxide, nutrients, hormones, wastes, immune molecules and clotting factors.

## Why This Matters

Without blood, oxygen from the lungs would not reach tissues, nutrients from digestion would not reach cells, hormones would not reach target organs, wastes would not reach excretory organs, and immune cells would not reach infection sites. Blood is therefore the mobile life-support network of the body.

## Blood-Specific Learning Focus

This lecture applies the central LBFL framework to blood composition and function. Learners should focus on plasma, formed elements, RBC specialization, WBC defence, platelet hemostasis, lymph return, and the connection between transport and immunity.

## Blood Composition Diagram

```text
Blood
├── Plasma ≈ 55%
│   ├── Water 90–92%
│   ├── Plasma proteins: albumin, globulin, fibrinogen
│   ├── Nutrients, electrolytes, hormones, enzymes
│   ├── Waste products and dissolved gases
│   └── CO₂ mainly as bicarbonate
└── Formed elements ≈ 45%
    ├── RBC / erythrocyte: oxygen transport
    ├── WBC / leukocyte: defence and immunity
    │   ├── Granulocytes: neutrophil, eosinophil, basophil
    │   └── Agranulocytes: lymphocyte, monocyte
    └── Platelet / thrombocyte: clotting and hemostasis
```

## Plasma

Plasma is the pale yellow liquid part of blood. It contains water, plasma proteins, nutrients, electrolytes, hormones, enzymes, wastes and gases.

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Water | Solvent, volume and heat transport |
| Albumin | Osmotic pressure and carrier function |
| Globulin | Immunity and transport |
| Fibrinogen | Precursor of clot formation |
| Electrolytes | pH, osmotic balance and membrane function |
| Nutrients | Food material transport |
| Wastes | Transport to kidney, liver or lungs |
| Bicarbonate | Major form of CO₂ transport |

## RBC: Oxygen Transport Specialist

RBC contains hemoglobin. Mature human RBC is biconcave, flexible and lacks a nucleus. These features increase surface area, allow capillary passage and provide more space for hemoglobin.

```text
Lungs: Hb + O₂ → HbO₂
Tissues: HbO₂ → Hb + O₂
```

RBC normally lives for about 120 days. It is produced mainly in red bone marrow.

## WBC: Defence and Immunity

WBC protects the body against infection, parasites, allergens and abnormal cells.

| Type | Main role |
|---|---|
| Neutrophil | Phagocytosis, especially bacterial defence |
| Eosinophil | Parasite defence and allergy association |
| Basophil | Histamine and allergic inflammation |
| Lymphocyte | Adaptive immunity; B cells, T cells and NK cells |
| Monocyte | Becomes macrophage; phagocytosis and cleanup |

## Platelets: Hemostasis and Clotting

Platelets are small cell fragments. When a blood vessel is injured, platelets adhere to the damaged site, form a temporary plug and support fibrin clot formation.

```text
Vessel injury
  ↓
Platelet adhesion
  ↓
Platelet plug
  ↓
Clotting factor activation
  ↓
Fibrin clot
  ↓
Bleeding reduced
```

## Lymph

Lymph is a pale, clear fluid derived from tissue fluid. It contains lymphocytes but normally lacks RBC and platelets. It returns excess tissue fluid to blood, helps immune surveillance and absorbs fat from intestinal villi through lacteals.
