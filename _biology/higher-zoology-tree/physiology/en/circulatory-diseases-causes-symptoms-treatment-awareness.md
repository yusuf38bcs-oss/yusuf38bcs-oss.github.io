# Blood Circulation Part 3: Circulatory Diseases, Causes, Symptoms and Treatment Awareness

## Medical Safety Boundary

This lecture is for educational understanding only. It is not medical diagnosis, prescription or emergency instruction. Chest pain, breathlessness, fainting, weakness of one side of the body, severe sweating, confusion or suspected heart attack requires urgent medical care.

## Concept Overview

Circulatory diseases usually occur when blood flow, vessel structure, heart rhythm, blood pressure or blood composition becomes abnormal. A disease may begin silently in the vessel wall, in blood components or in heart muscle. Over time, plaque deposition, clot formation, high pressure, poor oxygen supply or weak pumping can damage organs.

The most important learning rule is:

```text
Cause → Structural change → Blood-flow problem → Tissue oxygen problem → Symptoms → Treatment awareness
```

## Why This Matters

A student should not memorize “angina” and “heart attack” as isolated names. Angina means heart muscle receives insufficient oxygen temporarily. Heart attack means prolonged blockage causes myocardial cell death. Hypertension damages vessel walls. Anaemia reduces oxygen-carrying capacity. Thrombosis blocks blood flow. These diseases become clear when they are connected to circulation failure.

## LOLO: Learning Objectives and Learning Outcomes

### Learning Objectives

Learners will study how to:

1. Explain atherosclerosis as plaque formation inside arteries.
2. Distinguish angina from myocardial infarction.
3. Explain how coronary artery blockage reduces oxygen supply to heart muscle.
4. Connect hypertension with vessel-wall stress and heart workload.
5. Explain anaemia and polycythemia using RBC logic.
6. Explain thrombosis as abnormal clot formation inside vessels.
7. Identify warning signs that require medical care.
8. Understand treatment awareness without self-medication.

### Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lecture, learners will be able to:

- Build a disease comparison table.
- Explain why coronary plaque causes chest pain.
- Compare angina and heart attack.
- Explain why high blood pressure silently damages vessels.
- Connect anaemia with fatigue and poor oxygen delivery.
- Recognize the need for early medical consultation.

## Atherosclerosis

Atherosclerosis is a gradual disease in which lipid-rich plaque builds up inside artery walls. The plaque can narrow the lumen, reduce blood flow and become unstable.

```text
Endothelial injury / risk factors
  ↓
LDL entry and inflammation
  ↓
Plaque formation
  ↓
Narrowed artery lumen
  ↓
Reduced blood flow
  ↓
Ischemia or clot-related event
```

## Angina

Angina is chest pain or discomfort that occurs when heart muscle receives less oxygen-rich blood than it needs. It commonly appears during exertion, stress, heavy meals or cold exposure, because the heart demands more oxygen in those conditions.

### Angina logic

```text
Coronary artery narrowing
  ↓
Blood flow cannot meet myocardial demand
  ↓
Temporary ischemia
  ↓
Chest pain or discomfort
```

Angina may feel like pressure, heaviness, tightness or pain in the chest, and may radiate to shoulder, arm, neck or jaw. Any new or severe chest pain needs medical assessment.

## Heart Attack / Myocardial Infarction

A heart attack occurs when blood flow to a part of the heart muscle is severely reduced or blocked long enough to cause myocardial cell injury or death.

```text
Coronary plaque rupture or severe blockage
  ↓
Platelet aggregation and clot formation
  ↓
Oxygen-rich blood cannot reach myocardium
  ↓
Heart muscle cells are damaged or die
  ↓
Myocardial infarction
```

## Angina vs Heart Attack

| Feature | Angina | Heart Attack |
|---|---|---|
| Main problem | Temporary oxygen shortage | Prolonged blockage and muscle damage |
| Cell death | Usually absent | Present or developing |
| Duration | Often shorter | Often longer and more severe |
| Emergency level | Needs evaluation | Medical emergency |
| Treatment goal | Improve flow and reduce demand | Restore flow urgently and prevent complications |

## Hypertension

Hypertension means persistently high blood pressure. It increases stress on artery walls and heart muscle. Over time, it can damage vessels, kidney, brain and heart.

```text
High pressure
  ↓
Vessel wall stress
  ↓
Endothelial damage
  ↓
Atherosclerosis risk increases
  ↓
Heart workload increases
```

## Anaemia and Polycythemia

| Condition | Blood logic | Possible effect |
|---|---|---|
| Anaemia | RBC or hemoglobin too low | Reduced oxygen delivery, fatigue, weakness |
| Polycythemia | RBC mass too high | Increased viscosity and flow difficulty risk |

## Thrombosis

Thrombosis is clot formation inside a blood vessel. Normal clotting stops bleeding after injury. Thrombosis is dangerous because it can block blood flow inside an artery or vein.

## Red-Flag Symptoms

Seek urgent medical help for:

- Severe or persistent chest pain
- Chest pressure with sweating, nausea or breathlessness
- Pain radiating to left arm, shoulder, jaw or back
- Sudden weakness, facial droop or speech difficulty
- Fainting or severe dizziness
- Sudden severe shortness of breath

## Synaptic Bridge

Circulatory disease teaches that damage often begins silently. The same is true in life: small blockages in habit, thought and discipline can accumulate until crisis appears. Biology encourages early correction before damage becomes irreversible.

## Critical Thinking Questions

1. Why is angina a warning sign even if the pain stops?
2. Why can a normal clot save life but an abnormal clot threaten life?
3. How does high blood pressure promote vascular disease even without pain?
4. Why is myocardial infarction more dangerous than stable angina?
