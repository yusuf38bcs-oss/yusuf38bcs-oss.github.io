# Energy Flow and Ecological Pyramids

This Lecture 21 forms part of the canonical 29-lecture LBFL Ecology course. It develops energy flow and ecological pyramids through ecological mechanisms, evidence, interpretation and application.

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish gross primary production, net primary production, and respiration.
- Explain trophic transfer and why energy dissipates as heat.
- Compare pyramids of energy, biomass, and numbers.
- Interpret differences between standing biomass and productivity.

## 1. Energy enters and dissipates

Most ecosystems ultimately depend on solar energy. Photosynthetic organisms capture only a fraction of incoming radiation and store it as chemical energy. Organisms use chemical energy in metabolism, and much of it is eventually dissipated as heat. Nutrient atoms can cycle, but usable energy must be supplied continuously.

## 4. Trophic transfer efficiency

Only part of the production at one trophic level becomes production at the next. The familiar “10% rule” is a rough teaching heuristic, not a universal ecological constant. Real transfer efficiencies vary widely.

## 5. Ecological pyramids

Energy pyramids represent energy flow per unit area per unit time and are upright because energy is lost as heat at each transfer. Biomass pyramids can sometimes be inverted in aquatic systems because phytoplankton have low standing biomass but very rapid turnover.

Standing biomass is therefore not the same thing as productivity.

## Productivity and trophic transfer

\\[
NPP=GPP-R_a
\\]

where \\(R_a\\) is autotrophic respiration.

\\[
TE=\frac{P_{n+1}}{P_n}\times100
\\]

where \\(P_n\\) and \\(P_{n+1}\\) are production at consecutive trophic levels.

## Bangladesh Context

Productivity in floodplains, rice fields, ponds, haors, and mangroves changes with season, water depth, nutrients, and light. Fish yield may respond to primary productivity, but food-web structure and harvest pressure also matter.

## Application Exercise

If producer production is 10,000 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹ and herbivore production is 1,200 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹, calculate trophic transfer efficiency and explain why it need not equal 10%.

## Common Misconceptions

- Energy does not recycle through an ecosystem in the same way as nutrients.
- Standing biomass and productivity are not the same measure.
