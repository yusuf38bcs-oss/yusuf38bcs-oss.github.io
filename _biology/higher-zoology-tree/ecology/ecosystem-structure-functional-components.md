# Ecosystem Structure and Functional Components

This Lecture 19 forms part of the canonical 29-lecture LBFL Ecology course. It develops ecosystem structure and functional components through ecological mechanisms, evidence, interpretation and application.

## Learning Objectives

- Integrate biotic and abiotic components in the ecosystem concept.
- Explain the roles of producers, consumers, detritivores, and decomposers.
- Compare grazing and detrital food chains.
- Explain why food webs are more realistic than isolated food chains.

## 1. Ecosystem as a functional unit

An ecosystem combines a biological community with its physical and chemical environment. Organisms and the abiotic environment are linked by energy flow, consumption, decomposition, and the cycling of chemical elements.

## 2. Trophic levels

Primary producers capture solar or chemical energy and convert it to organic matter. Herbivores consume producers, and predators consume other animals. Omnivores feed at more than one trophic level, so real ecosystems do not form perfectly ordered ladders.

## Bangladesh Context

Ponds and wetlands contain grazing and detrital pathways at the same time. Mangrove leaf litter can also enter estuarine detrital food webs, linking terrestrial vegetation with aquatic consumers.

## Application Exercise

Build a ten-node pond food web using resource-to-consumer arrows. Mark the detrital pathway and include at least one omnivore.

## Common Misconceptions

- Decomposers and detritivores are not exactly the same functional group.
- An organism can change trophic role during its life cycle.
