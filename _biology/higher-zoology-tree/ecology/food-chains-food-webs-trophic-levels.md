# Food Chains, Food Webs and Trophic Levels

This Lecture 20 forms part of the canonical 29-lecture LBFL Ecology course. It develops food chains, food webs and trophic levels through ecological mechanisms, evidence, interpretation and application.

{% include education/framework-links.html %}

## Learning Objectives and Outcomes — LOLO

- Integrate biotic and abiotic components in the ecosystem concept.
- Explain the roles of producers, consumers, detritivores, and decomposers.
- Compare grazing and detrital food chains.
- Explain why food webs are more realistic than isolated food chains.

## 2. Trophic levels

Primary producers capture solar or chemical energy and convert it to organic matter. Herbivores consume producers, and predators consume other animals. Omnivores feed at more than one trophic level, so real ecosystems do not form perfectly ordered ladders.

## 3. Grazing food chains

A grazing pathway begins with living plant or algal tissue:

```text
phytoplankton → zooplankton → small fish → larger fish
```

This simplified chain is especially useful in aquatic examples, but each node usually has multiple consumers and resources.

## 4. Detrital food chains

A detrital pathway begins with dead organic matter. Detritivores fragment or ingest detritus, while bacteria and fungi chemically decompose complex organic compounds. Forest, soil, wetland, and mangrove ecosystems often depend strongly on detrital pathways.

## 5. Food webs

Food webs connect many food chains. Omnivory, scavenging, seasonal diet shifts, and life-stage changes make the network more complex. Food-web analysis helps explain indirect effects, alternative energy pathways, and the consequences of species loss.

## Bangladesh Context

Ponds and wetlands contain grazing and detrital pathways at the same time. Mangrove leaf litter can also enter estuarine detrital food webs, linking terrestrial vegetation with aquatic consumers.

## Learning Activity and Application — LALA

Build a ten-node pond food web using resource-to-consumer arrows. Mark the detrital pathway and include at least one omnivore.

## Common Misconceptions

- Decomposers and detritivores are not exactly the same functional group.
- An organism can change trophic role during its life cycle.
