# Adaptation, Acclimatization and Environmental Response

This Lecture 05 forms part of the canonical 29-lecture LBFL Ecology course. It develops adaptation, acclimatization and environmental response through ecological mechanisms, evidence, interpretation and application.


## Key Ideas

- Distinguish additive, synergistic, and antagonistic effects among environmental factors.
- Apply the idea of primary and secondary limiting factors.
- Compare morphological, physiological, and behavioural adaptations.
- Distinguish acclimation, phenotypic plasticity, and evolutionary adaptation.

## 1. Multiple-factor ecology

Organisms do not experience one environmental factor at a time. Temperature, humidity, salinity, food, predators, disease, and disturbance operate together. A single-factor experiment may therefore fail to predict field performance.

An **additive effect** approximates the sum of individual effects. A **synergistic effect** is stronger than expected from that sum. An **antagonistic effect** occurs when one factor reduces the effect of another. High temperature combined with low humidity, for example, can create unusually strong desiccation stress in arthropods.

## 2. Hierarchy of limiting factors

Removing a **primary limitation** often reveals a **secondary limitation**. Nutrient enrichment may initially increase algal production, after which light limitation or grazing may become more important. Management fails when it targets a factor that is not currently limiting.

## 3. Major forms of adaptation

**Morphological adaptations** involve structure, such as thick cuticles or pneumatophores. **Physiological adaptations** involve internal function, such as salt secretion or concentrated urine. **Behavioural adaptations** involve actions such as burrowing, nocturnality, migration, or basking.

## 4. Plasticity and evolutionary change

Phenotypic plasticity allows the same genotype to produce different phenotypes in different environments. Evolutionary adaptation requires heritable variation and differential reproductive success across generations. Plasticity can be beneficial, neutral, or costly; it is not automatically adaptive.

## Integrated Perspective

### 5. Acclimation and adaptation

**Acclimation** is a reversible adjustment within an individual's lifetime. **Evolutionary adaptation** is a heritable population-level change produced by selection across generations. Confusing these processes leads to weak ecological explanations.

## Bangladesh Context

Coastal organisms may face heat, humidity, salinity, tidal inundation, food variation, and disturbance simultaneously. Dry-season freshwater fish can experience a different combination: low water depth, high temperature, crowding, and low dissolved oxygen.

## Application and Practice

Analyze a hypothetical insect that survives well at 30°C with high humidity, less well at 35°C, and very poorly at 35°C with low humidity. Explain the possible synergy and propose morphological, physiological, and behavioural responses.

## Common Misconceptions

- A useful trait is not automatically an evolutionary adaptation without evidence of heritable selection history.
- The ranking of primary and secondary limiting factors can change over time.
