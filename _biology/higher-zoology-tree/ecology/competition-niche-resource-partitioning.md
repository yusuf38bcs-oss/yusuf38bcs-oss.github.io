# Competition, Niche Overlap and Resource Partitioning

This Lecture 14 forms part of the canonical 29-lecture LBFL Ecology course. It develops competition, niche overlap and resource partitioning through ecological mechanisms, evidence, interpretation and application.

{% include education/framework-links.html %}

## Learning Objectives and Outcomes — LOLO

- Explain the competitive exclusion principle and its assumptions.
- Describe spatial, dietary, and temporal resource partitioning.
- Evaluate evidence for character displacement.
- Connect allopatry, sympatry, and biological clocks to species interactions.

## 1. Competitive exclusion

If two species use the same limiting resources in essentially identical ways under stable conditions, indefinite coexistence is unlikely. The competitive exclusion principle is best used as a hypothesis about coexistence, not as a universal deterministic rule.

## 2. Resource partitioning

Coexisting species can differ in resource use. Partitioning may be **spatial** (canopy versus ground), **dietary** (different prey sizes), **temporal** (day versus night), or based on microhabitat. Such differences can reduce interspecific competition.

A partitioning pattern does not by itself prove that current competition created it. It may reflect evolutionary history.

## 3. Character displacement

Character displacement is supported when ecologically relevant traits diverge more strongly where similar species occur together than where they occur separately. Strong evidence must rule out alternative explanations such as habitat differences and shared ancestry.

## 4. Allopatry and sympatry

**Allopatry** means geographic separation; **sympatry** means overlapping geographic ranges. Direct interspecific competition requires ecological contact, so allopatry removes that opportunity while sympatry creates the possibility of interaction.

## Integrated Perspective

### 2. Hutchinson's niche concept

A niche can be represented as an n-dimensional hypervolume. Dimensions can include temperature, salinity, prey size, nesting substrate, water depth, activity time, and other biologically relevant variables.

### 3. Fundamental and realized niche

The **fundamental niche** represents the range a species could occupy based on its physiological and resource requirements in the absence of restrictive biotic interactions. The **realized niche** reflects the range actually occupied after competition, predation, disease, dispersal constraints, and other interactions are considered.

### 4. Niche breadth and overlap

Generalists use a broad range of resources or conditions; specialists use a narrower range. High overlap creates the potential for competition, but overlap alone does not prove competition. Resources may be abundant or interactions may be weak.

## Bangladesh Context

Intertidal crabs, estuarine fish, mosquitoes, bats, and seasonal breeders provide examples of daily, tidal, and seasonal biological timing. Harvest that concentrates during spawning periods can turn timing into a management issue.

## Learning Activity and Application — LALA

Two similar bird species feed at different forest heights. Write a resource-partitioning hypothesis and list the additional evidence needed before claiming character displacement.

## Common Misconceptions

- Observed resource partitioning does not prove that current competition created it.
- Biological clocks include tidal, lunar, and seasonal rhythms as well as daily rhythms.
