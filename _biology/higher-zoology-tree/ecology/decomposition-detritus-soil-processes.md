# Decomposition, Detritus and Soil Processes

This Lecture 23 forms part of the canonical 29-lecture LBFL Ecology course. It develops decomposition, detritus and soil processes through ecological mechanisms, evidence, interpretation and application.

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish reservoirs from fluxes in biogeochemical cycles.
- Trace major pathways in carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, sulfur, and water cycles.
- Distinguish nitrogen fixation, nitrification, mineralization, assimilation, and denitrification.
- Explain how human activities alter nutrient cycling and eutrophication risk.

## 1. Reservoirs and fluxes

A **reservoir** or pool is a place where an element is stored. A **flux** is the rate at which material moves between pools. A large pool does not necessarily cycle quickly; residence time depends on both pool size and outflow rate.

## 6. Decomposition and nutrient dynamics

Decomposition depends on litter chemistry, temperature, moisture, oxygen, and the decomposer community. Mineralization releases inorganic nutrients; microbial immobilization temporarily stores nutrients in biomass.

## Residence time

A useful steady-state approximation is:

\[
\tau\approx\frac{\text{pool size}}{\text{outflow rate}}
\]

It should be interpreted carefully when pools and fluxes are rapidly changing.

## Integrated Perspective

### 4. Detrital food chains

A detrital pathway begins with dead organic matter. Detritivores fragment or ingest detritus, while bacteria and fungi chemically decompose complex organic compounds. Forest, soil, wetland, and mangrove ecosystems often depend strongly on detrital pathways.

## Integrated Perspective

## 2. Soil as an ecological system

Soil is a dynamic mixture of mineral particles, organic matter, air, water, and living organisms. **Texture** describes the proportions of sand, silt, and clay, while **structure** describes how particles form aggregates. Clay-rich soils often retain more water and ions but may drain slowly. Sandy soils drain rapidly.

Soil pH changes nutrient solubility, microbial activity, and toxicity. Organic matter improves nutrient retention, water-holding capacity, and the resource base for decomposers.

## Bangladesh Context

Fertilizer runoff and sewage can increase nitrogen and phosphorus loading in ponds and wetlands. Flood pulses can redistribute nutrients across large areas, while decomposition can alter oxygen conditions.

## Application Exercise

Trace one nitrogen atom from fertilizer through a plant, consumer, detritus, ammonium, nitrate, and finally atmospheric nitrogen. Name the process at each transformation.

## Common Misconceptions

- The carbon cycle includes oceans, soils, sediments, and combustion, not only photosynthesis and respiration.
- Nitrogen transformations occur under different chemical and oxygen conditions.
