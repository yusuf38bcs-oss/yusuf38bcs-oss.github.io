# Carbon, Nitrogen, Phosphorus and Water Cycles

This Lecture 24 forms part of the canonical 29-lecture LBFL Ecology course. It develops carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus and water cycles through ecological mechanisms, evidence, interpretation and application.

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish reservoirs from fluxes in biogeochemical cycles.
- Trace major pathways in carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, sulfur, and water cycles.
- Distinguish nitrogen fixation, nitrification, mineralization, assimilation, and denitrification.
- Explain how human activities alter nutrient cycling and eutrophication risk.

## 1. Reservoirs and fluxes

A **reservoir** or pool is a place where an element is stored. A **flux** is the rate at which material moves between pools. A large pool does not necessarily cycle quickly; residence time depends on both pool size and outflow rate.

## 2. Carbon cycle

Atmospheric carbon dioxide is fixed by photosynthesis and returned through respiration and decomposition. Biomass, soils, oceans, sediments, rocks, and fossil carbon are major reservoirs with very different turnover times. Combustion and land-use change alter carbon fluxes.

## 3. Nitrogen cycle

Atmospheric nitrogen gas is unavailable to most organisms without transformation. **Nitrogen fixation** converts \\(N_2\\) into biologically available forms. **Nitrification** converts reduced nitrogen to nitrate. **Assimilation** incorporates inorganic nitrogen into biomass. **Mineralization** releases ammonium from organic matter. **Denitrification** converts nitrate to gaseous nitrogen compounds under suitable conditions.

## 4. Phosphorus cycle

Phosphorus has no major atmospheric gaseous phase. Weathering releases phosphate, which is taken up by organisms, transferred through food webs, recycled through decomposition, and eventually stored in sediments. Excess phosphorus can contribute to freshwater eutrophication.

## 5. Sulfur and water cycles

Sulfur moves among rocks, oceans, the atmosphere, and organisms, with microbial oxidation and reduction playing important roles. The hydrological cycle links evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, and groundwater flow.

## Residence time

A useful steady-state approximation is:

\\[
\tau\approx\frac{\text{pool size}}{\text{outflow rate}}
\\]

It should be interpreted carefully when pools and fluxes are rapidly changing.

## Bangladesh Context

Fertilizer runoff and sewage can increase nitrogen and phosphorus loading in ponds and wetlands. Flood pulses can redistribute nutrients across large areas, while decomposition can alter oxygen conditions.

## Application Exercise

Trace one nitrogen atom from fertilizer through a plant, consumer, detritus, ammonium, nitrate, and finally atmospheric nitrogen. Name the process at each transformation.

## Common Misconceptions

- The carbon cycle includes oceans, soils, sediments, and combustion, not only photosynthesis and respiration.
- Nitrogen transformations occur under different chemical and oxygen conditions.
