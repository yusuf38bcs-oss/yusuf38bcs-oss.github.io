A biological community consists of populations of different species that occur together and interact. Community ecology examines competition, predation, herbivory, parasitism, mutualism, commensalism, facilitation, species diversity, dominance, succession, disturbance, and the organization of interaction networks.

Community patterns arise from both biological interactions and environmental filtering. The presence or abundance of one species can affect others directly through feeding or competition, indirectly through food-web pathways, or by altering habitat structure. Interpretation therefore requires attention to scale, history, abiotic conditions, and the strengths of multiple interacting processes.
