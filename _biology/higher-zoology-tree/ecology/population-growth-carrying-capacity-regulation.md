# Population Growth, Carrying Capacity and Regulation

This Lecture 09 forms part of the canonical 29-lecture LBFL Ecology course. It develops population growth, carrying capacity and regulation through ecological mechanisms, evidence, interpretation and application.

## Learning Objectives

- Interpret Type I, Type II, and Type III survivorship curves.
- Compare exponential and logistic population-growth models.
- Interpret intrinsic rate of increase, \\(r\\), and carrying capacity, \\(K\\).
- Identify assumptions and limitations of simple population models.

## 2. Exponential growth

When resources are effectively unlimited and the per-capita rate of increase remains constant, population growth can be exponential. Growth begins slowly at small population size and accelerates as the number of reproducing individuals increases.

## 3. Logistic growth

The logistic model introduces density dependence through a carrying capacity, \\(K\\). As population size approaches \\(K\\), resource limitation or other density-dependent processes reduce per-capita growth.

Carrying capacity is not an immutable number for a species. It changes with habitat quality, climate, resource supply, disease, and disturbance.

## 4. Models are simplifications

The exponential model can approximate the early phase of invasion or recovery. The logistic model captures density limitation. Neither automatically includes seasonality, age structure, time lags, environmental stochasticity, or species interactions. Models should be judged by the question they are designed to answer.

## Core growth equations

\\[
\frac{dN}{dt}=rN,\qquad N_t=N_0e^{rt}
\\]

\\[
\frac{dN}{dt}=rN\left(1-\frac{N}{K}\right)
\\]

The first describes exponential growth; the second introduces density dependence through carrying capacity \\(K\\).

## Integrated Perspective

### 1. Density dependence

Competition, disease transmission, territorial conflict, and some forms of predation often intensify as population density rises. When per-capita population growth declines with density, the resulting negative feedback can regulate population size.

### 2. Density-independent forces

Cyclones, floods, fire, extreme temperatures, and toxic spills can cause major mortality without depending directly on population density. Even so, exposure can still vary among habitats or social groups, so the label should be used carefully.

### 3. Limitation versus regulation

A limiting factor reduces growth or performance. A regulating process creates density-dependent feedback that tends to push population size toward a characteristic range. Every regulator limits growth at some stage, but not every limiting factor regulates a population.

## Bangladesh Context

Population models can be applied cautiously to aquaculture stocking, mosquito outbreaks, invasive aquatic plants, or recovering wildlife populations. The model assumptions should be checked before management decisions are based on the prediction.

## Application Exercise

For \\(N_0=100\\), \\(r=0.2\,yr^{-1}\\), and \\(t=3\\) years, calculate exponential growth and then list the assumptions that make the prediction biologically plausible or implausible.

## Common Misconceptions

- Type III survivorship and r-selection are related teaching ideas but are not identical concepts.
- Carrying capacity is environment-dependent, not a permanent species constant.
