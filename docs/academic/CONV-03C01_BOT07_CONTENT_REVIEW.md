## Scientific references

### Reference A — NCBI Bookshelf: The Plant Cell Wall

URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26928/

Used to verify:

- plant cell wall as an extracellular structure;
- cellulose-rich wall architecture;
- wall tensile strength;
- relationship between cell wall and turgor pressure;
- wall-supported plant-cell expansion.

### Reference B — NCBI Bookshelf: Cell Walls and the Extracellular Matrix

URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9874/

Used to verify:

- primary vs secondary wall distinction;
- cellulose, hemicellulose and pectin as major plant-wall components;
- stronger/layered secondary walls;
- turgor pressure and resistance to osmotic swelling;
- central-vacuole contribution to cell expansion.

### Reference C — NCBI Bookshelf: Plant and Fungal Vacuoles Are Remarkably Versatile Lysosomes

URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26844/

Used to verify:

- vacuoles as large membrane-bound compartments;
- tonoplast terminology;
- storage, degradation and homeostatic functions;
- role in turgor pressure;
- vacuolar contribution to cell-size control.

### Reference D — OpenStax Biology 2e: Eukaryotic Cells

URL: https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells

Used as HSC-level cross-check for:

- central vacuole as a characteristic plant-cell structure;
- wall and vacuole roles in support and pressure.

## Wording controls

The lesson deliberately avoids these overstatements:

- “all plant cell walls are lignified”;
- “cell wall is selectively permeable like plasma membrane”;
- “vacuole is an empty space”;
- “every plant cell always has one huge central vacuole”;
- “one private textbook edition defines the canonical wording”.

The lesson uses qualified wording such as “many secondary walls”, “mature plant cell”, and “can store” where biological variation matters.
