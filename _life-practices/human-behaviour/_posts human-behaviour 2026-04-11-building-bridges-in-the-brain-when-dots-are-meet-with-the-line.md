    <div class="bio-header">
        <div class="bio-category">Neurobiology & Learning Sciences</div>
        <h1>Building Bridges in Your Brain 🧠</h1>
        <p>The Biological Foundation of Memory & Cognitive Mastery</p>
    </div>

    <details open>
        <summary>I. The Construction Site in Your Head</summary>
        <div class="content-body">
            <p>Learning isn't abstract; it's physical. Your head is a construction site where <strong>Neurons</strong> <span class="bn-meaning">(স্নায়ুকোষ)</span> act as hubs, while <strong>Axons</strong> <span class="bn-meaning">(অ্যাক্সন)</span> and <strong>Dendrites</strong> <span class="bn-meaning">(ডেনড্রাইট)</span> serve as the wiring.</p>
            
            

[Image of the structure of a neuron and synapse]

            
            <p>The "magic" happens at the <span class="tech-term">Synaptic Cleft</span> <span class="bn-meaning">(সিন্যাপটিক ক্লেফট)</span>. Through a process called <strong>Long-Term Potentiation (LTP)</strong>, these bridges are strengthened. We aren't just filling a container; we are reinforcing physical paths for electrical signals.</p>
        </div>
    </details>

    <details>
        <summary>II. Battle Royale: Web vs. Lego</summary>
        <div class="content-body">
            <p>In the 19th century, scientists fought over how our brains were wired:</p>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Team Web (Golgi):</strong> Thought the brain was one fused mess (Reticular Theory).</li>
                <li><strong>Team Lego (Cajal):</strong> Proved the <span class="tech-term">Neuron Doctrine</span> <span class="bn-meaning">(স্নায়ুতত্ত্ব)</span>—neurons are individual units that "clasp" together but don't fuse.</li>
            </ul>
            <p>In 1897, <strong>Sir Charles Sherrington</strong> named this connection the <strong>"Synapse"</strong> <span class="bn-meaning">(স্নায়ুসন্ধি)</span>.</p>
        </div>
    </details>

    <details>
        <summary>III. The Synapse Audit: Applied Practices</summary>
        <div class="content-body">
            <p>Move from theory to mental mastery with these biological audits:</p>

            <div class="research-box">
                <h3>Practice 1: The LTP Reinforcement</h3>
                <ul>
                    <li><strong>Spaced Repetition:</strong> Cross the bridge at intervals. This prevents <span class="tech-term">Microglia</span> <span class="bn-meaning">(মাইক্রোগ্লিয়া)</span> from "pruning" or deleting the connection.</li>
                    <li><strong>Active Recall:</strong> Force the <span class="tech-term">Action Potential</span> <span class="bn-meaning">(সক্রিয় বিভব)</span> to fire. Testing yourself strengthens the bridge better than passive reading.</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <div class="research-box">
                <h3>Practice 2: The Glial Support System</h3>
                <ul>
                    <li><strong>Sleep for Pruning:</strong> During deep sleep, your <span class="tech-term">Astrocytes</span> <span class="bn-meaning">(অ্যাস্ট্রোসাইট)</span> clean metabolic waste. No sleep = clogged bridges.</li>
                    <li><strong>Predictive Coding:</strong> Visualize your goal to prime your neural circuits and reduce "prediction error."</li>
                </ul>
            </div>
        </div>
    </details>

    <details>
        <summary>IV. The Engram & Future Horizons</summary>
        <div class="content-body">
            <p>Scientists hunt for the <span class="tech-term">Engram</span> <span class="bn-meaning">(স্মৃতিচিহ্ন)</span>—the physical trace of memory. Using <strong>Optogenetics</strong>, we've learned that "forgotten" memories often still exist; we've just lost the keys.</p>
            
            <p>We are entering the era of <strong>Connectomics</strong> and <strong>Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs)</strong>. Tools like <strong>synaptoPAC</strong> can now use blue light to literally "write" a memory in real-time.</p>
        </div>
    </details>
